01 · Estate planning

Decide who gets what, and who’s in charge if you can’t be.

An estate plan is a short stack of papers that says what happens to your things when you die, and who can make decisions for you if you’re sick or hurt. Most families need four documents. We’ll explain each one before you sign anything.

Free consult Call us

Mon–Fri 9–5 · evenings by appointment

  • A willSays who inherits your things, and who would raise your children if you couldn’t.
  • A trust, if it helpsLets your family skip most of probate and keeps the details private. Not everyone needs one; we’ll tell you honestly if you don’t.
  • A health care proxyNames one person to make medical decisions for you if you can’t speak for yourself.
  • A durable power of attorneyLets someone you trust pay your bills and handle money matters if you’re unable to.

A good time to call

  • You had a baby, got married, or got divorced
  • You bought a house or moved to Massachusetts
  • Your current plan is more than five years old
  • You don’t have a plan at all (most people don’t)

02 · Elder law

Plan for long-term care before it becomes an emergency.

A year in a Massachusetts nursing home can cost more than many families save in a lifetime. MassHealth (our state’s Medicaid program) can help pay, but its rules about savings, the house, and gifts to family are strict. We help you understand them and plan ahead.

  • MassHealth planningWhat counts against you, what doesn’t, and how the five-year “look-back” on gifts works.
  • MassHealth applicationsWe gather the paperwork, file the application, and answer the follow-up letters so you don’t have to.
  • Protecting the family homeOptions that may help keep the house in the family, explained with their trade-offs.
  • Guardianship and conservatorshipIf a parent can no longer decide for themselves and no plan is in place, we ask the court to put someone in charge.

A good time to call

  • A parent needs more help at home than they used to
  • Someone is heading into rehab or a nursing home
  • A MassHealth letter asked for more documents, or said no

Every family is different. We’ll tell you plainly what is and isn’t possible in your situation.

03 · Probate

Settling an estate, one step at a time.

When someone dies, their property often has to pass through the Probate and Family Court before it can go to the family. It’s mostly paperwork and deadlines, at a time when you have enough to carry. We handle the court side and keep you posted.

Free consult Call us

Mon–Fri 9–5 · evenings by appointment

  1. Open the estateWe file the will (if there is one) and the first forms with the court.
  2. Name who’s in chargeThe court appoints a “personal representative,” usually the person named in the will.
  3. Take stock and pay billsWe list what the person owned, notify creditors, and pay final bills and taxes.
  4. Distribute and closeWhat’s left goes to the family, and we file the papers that close the estate.

Many simple estates wrap up in about a year. After we look at yours, we’ll give you a realistic timeline and a written fee.

Plain answers

Questions people ask us

Short, honest answers. If yours isn’t here, ask us. There’s no such thing as a silly question about your family’s future.

How much does a will or estate plan cost?

It depends on what you need, so we don’t guess on the phone. After your free consult we quote a flat fee in writing for most plans. You only pay if you decide to go ahead.

Do I need a trust, or is a will enough?

Plenty of people only need a will, a health care proxy and a power of attorney. A trust can help if you own a home, want to keep things private, or want to spare your family most of probate. We’ll tell you which camp you’re in.

What is MassHealth’s five-year look-back?

When someone applies for MassHealth to help pay for nursing home care, the state looks at gifts and transfers made in the five years before. Some of those can delay coverage. That’s why planning early matters.

Does every estate have to go through probate?

No. Things held in a trust, owned jointly, or with a named beneficiary usually pass outside probate. Small estates may qualify for a simpler process. We’ll look at what your loved one owned and tell you which path applies.

Can you meet my parent at home or in a nursing home?

Yes. We regularly visit clients at home, in the hospital, and in assisted living around Hampden, Hampshire and Franklin counties. Just ask when you call.

Does contacting you make you my lawyer?

Not yet. We become your lawyers once we both sign a written engagement letter. Until then, please don’t send us private or sensitive details through this website.
